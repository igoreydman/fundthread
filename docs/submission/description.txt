FundThread — Keep the receipt. Follow the funds.

Nonprofit teams need to explain how restricted grant dollars were spent, but receipts, reimbursement decisions, payment records, and funder reports often live in separate places. A simple question — “How much is left, and what supports that number?” — becomes a search across documents and spreadsheets. Sharing the evidence can also expose personal information that a funder does not need.

FundThread connects that work in one receipt-first workspace. Staff see the recorded budget and remaining amount for each fund, add PDF or image receipts in a batch, and check locally extracted details against the preserved original. Drafts save automatically, but a person explicitly reviews the expense, assigns eligible amounts to funds, and records the reference for an external approval. Payments and bank reconciliation remain separate from approval and grant usage.

The working demo follows a complete example: a $200 workshop fund, two pending receipts, and one newly uploaded $53.25 receipt. After review and external approval, the fund shows $146.75 remaining. Recording a $20 external payment leaves $33.25 owed without charging the fund twice. Daily Analytics shows the approved expense, category, payments, outstanding amount, and the two receipts still needing review.

Reporting starts with the intended recipient. The funder profile omits claimant names, merchants, purpose, and approval identities; the bookkeeper profile includes detailed fields. Staff preview the report, check its fields and recipients, then export exact-dollar CSV or an organization-letterhead PDF. Receipt-copy tools can burn selected privacy masks into a new PDF while preserving the original. Minimal reports still contain IDs, dates, amounts, and fund codes, so they are not anonymous.

Built with React, TypeScript, Vite, PDF.js, Tesseract.js, pdf-lib, and a Tauri/Rust desktop core backed by PostgreSQL. English OCR assets are bundled and document reading runs locally. The browser demo needs no account, database, or API key; its workspace lasts only for the tab. Persistent desktop records use an organization-managed PostgreSQL database.

This is a working hackathon prototype, not a banking or approver-authentication service. It records decisions and payments made elsewhere. OCR and privacy suggestions require human review. PDFs contain rendered page images; CSV provides editable data. All demo organizations, people, receipts, and references are fictional.

Run instructions, source code, and the MIT license are in the public repository: https://github.com/igoreydman/fundthread
